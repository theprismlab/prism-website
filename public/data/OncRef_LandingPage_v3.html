About the Dataset

A Benchmark for Oncology Therapeutics

OncRef is generated using PRISM (Profiling Relative Inhibition Simultaneously in Mixtures), a high-throughput drug screening platform developed at the Broad Institute that introduces unique 24-nucleotide DNA barcodes into individual cancer cell lines, enabling pooled multiplexed screening. Each compound was profiled in triplicate 8-point dose-response.

The collection encompasses 172 agents (65%) not represented in other large-scale public datasets, spanning small molecules, protein degraders (PROTACs), and biologics including antibody-drug conjugates. Parallel genome-wide CRISPR knockout screens enable a new analytical framework that deconvolves polypharmacology, off-target effects, and on-target selectivity.

The Underlying Technology

The PRISM Platform — Multiplexed Cancer Drug Screening at Scale

OncRef is built on PRISM (Profiling Relative Inhibition Simultaneously in Mixtures), a high-throughput multiplexed viability screening platform developed at the Broad Institute. PRISM introduces unique 24-nucleotide DNA barcodes into cancer cell lines, enabling hundreds of cell lines to be screened simultaneously in a single well — dramatically reducing cost and increasing throughput compared to traditional approaches.

Vignettes from the Paper

Key Findings the Data Surfaced

OncRef goes beyond cataloging drug sensitivity. By correlating each compound's cell-line response profile with genome-wide CRISPR dependencies, the framework identifies on-target engagement, unexpected polypharmacology, and shared off-target liabilities. Click any figure to view it full-size.

01 On-target mapping

Half of inhibitors hit what they say they hit — at rank 1

For 50% of agents, the annotated target's genetic dependency is the single best predictor of sensitivity across the 891 cell lines. Another 10% have it within the top 10. The strength of this match often tracks with clinical trial success — drugs that fail this test get flagged for closer mechanistic inspection.

Figure 1D–E · Rank of annotated target among top correlations
02 KRAS landscape

A clear evolution in KRAS G12C selectivity

Divarasib, sotorasib, and adagrasib show progressively cleaner separation between KRAS G12C-mutant cell lines (red) and everything else (gray). Pan-RAS drugs sub-divide too: daraxonrasib and RMC-7977 favor NRAS/HRAS-mutant contexts, while BI-2865 and BI-2493 are more active in KRAS-amplified lines.

Figure 6C · AUC distributions in G12C-mutant vs. all other lines
03 Hidden polypharmacology

A 5-LOX inhibitor that actually hits TEAD1

BW-B70C, annotated as a lipoxygenase inhibitor, sat next to known TEAD inhibitors in profile space. Boltz2 structural co-folding plus molecular dynamics predicted a stable pose in TEAD1's lipid pocket (shown), with hydrogen bonds to S356/P357 maintained throughout the trajectory. A TEAD luciferase reporter confirmed submicromolar inhibition in MCF7 cells.

Figure 5G · Predicted BW-B70C pose in TEAD1 hydrophobic pocket
04 Shared off-target signature

A lysosomotropism cluster catches drugs in the act

BI-2493 (a KRAS inhibitor) killed cells without KRAS mutations — the profile correlated with chloroquine. Eight more agents (including adagrasib and crizotinib at higher doses) joined the same cluster. All carry protonatable amines and induce phospholipidosis, visible as green NBD-PE accumulation in lysosomal membranes.

Figure 7D · NBD-PE fluorescence in RVH421 melanoma cells

05 Filling the map

Hematopoietic and pediatric depth

The 891-line panel was deliberately expanded into underrepresented disease contexts — most notably hematopoietic malignancies and pediatric cancers — bringing the model collection closer to the genomic landscape of real patient tumors as visualized by DepMap's Celligner.

06 A reusable framework

Quantitative scores for every compound

The paper introduces a framework that converts the raw dose-response matrix into per-compound scores for specificity, on-target selectivity, on-target polypharmacology, and non-target effects. The same scoring can be applied to any new agent screened on the PRISM platform — making OncRef a comparator, not just a catalog.
